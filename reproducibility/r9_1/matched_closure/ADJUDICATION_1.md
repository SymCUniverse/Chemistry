# CSA R9.1 Matched Four-Layer Closure: Candidate Adjudication 1

Date: 2026-09-25
Branch: `csa-r9.1-matched-closure-search`
Protocol: `reproducibility/r9_1/matched_closure/PROTOCOL.md`

## Executive result

No candidate currently reaches `FULL_CLOSE` under the preregistered gates.

- CO/Cu(001): `CONDITIONAL_CLOSE / NEAR_MISS` boundary. It is the strongest candidate. The same lateral frustrated-translation coordinate is experimentally resolved, damped, and tied to microscopic surface diffusion, and independent DFT/spin-echo work supports a bridge-saddle diffusion barrier near 0.1 eV. The remaining blocker is a quantitatively condition-matched damping value and a frozen quantitative carrier-to-saddle overlap.
- Co2(CO)8: `NEAR_MISS`. Real 2D-IR chemical exchange, independent energetic calculations, and vibrational reporters exist, but the observed CO-stretch reporter has not been shown to be the reaction-coordinate carrier and exact same-mode T1 plus quantitative carrier-to-saddle projection have not been recovered.
- HONO/Kr: `NEAR_MISS`. Mode-selective chemistry and rich path dynamics are real, but the OH-stretch population loss is entangled with IVR/isomerization and therefore cannot be used as an independent mechanical damping input for the same reaction outcome without circular contamination.
- Photoinduced ET GLE (Angulo et al.): `REFUSED` for lowercase chi, but a successful native-dynamics control. The source requires a non-Markovian memory kernel on the solvent-polarization coordinate. Collapsing it to a local scalar damping ratio would violate the protocol.

## Candidate 1: CO/Cu(001)

### Successes
1. The isolated-adsorbate frustrated translation is experimentally assigned at 3.94 +/- 0.07 meV (Ellis et al. 1995, DOI 10.1063/1.469555).
2. The same mode has measured lifetime broadening; the 0 K extrapolated intrinsic width is 85 +/- 5 micro-eV, corresponding to 8 +/- 1 ps (Graham et al. 1996, DOI 10.1063/1.471260).
3. Under the CSA population-lifetime convention, the 0 K scalar is numerically
   [
   chi_0 = \frac{1/T_1}{2(E/\hbar)} = \frac{\hbar}{2ET_1} \approx 0.0104,
   ]
   equivalently approximately 0.0108 from width/(2 mode energy), within the quoted uncertainties and convention rounding.
4. The physical carrier is lateral frustrated translation, the same class of coordinate required for adsorbate hopping.
5. Independent periodic DFT finds atop minima, bridge diffusion saddles, and barriers of 95 +/- 30 and 125 +/- 30 meV depending on GGA (Fouquet et al. 2003, DOI 10.1063/1.1578054).
6. Later 3He spin-echo directly measures activated microscopic CO dynamics and obtains a nearly isotropic PES with an average barrier of approximately 125 meV, explicitly testing the first-principles PES (Alexandrowicz et al. 2004, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.93.156103).

### Failure / blocker
The early QHAS activation estimate of 31 +/- 10 meV is not sufficiently robust to serve as the independent barrier. Later DFT and higher-resolution spin-echo evidence cluster near 0.1-0.13 eV instead. The scalar damping value presently recovered numerically is the 0 K extrapolate, whereas diffusion is measured at finite temperature. A same-condition finite-temperature linewidth/lifetime has not yet been recovered numerically from a source table or full text. In addition, isotope work shows rotational admixture in the frustrated-translation mode, so reaction-coordinate participation should be computed rather than set to unity by name.

### Failure investigation
This is not a conceptual failure of CSA. It is primarily a condition/provenance closure problem plus a quantitative modal-projection gap. The literature already contains temperature-dependent lifetime theory for the same CO/Cu(100) modes from 0-450 K (Tully et al. 1993, DOI 10.1116/1.578522), and later theory treats phonon/electronic damping explicitly. A future full close is plausible if the finite-T numerical lifetime curve and mass-weighted saddle overlap are recovered or recomputed under a frozen surface model.

## Candidate 2: Co2(CO)8 bridged/nonbridged isomerization

### Successes
1. Ultrafast 2D-IR directly observes equilibrium population exchange between bridged and nonbridged isomers on picosecond timescales.
2. Temperature-dependent forward and reverse rates exist (Anna, Ross & Kubarych 2009, DOI 10.1021/jp903112c).
3. The solvent-friction study separates energetic from dynamic solvent contributions and compares the exchange with Kramers behavior (Anna & Kubarych 2010, DOI 10.1063/1.3492724).
4. Independent quantum-chemical literature establishes multiple low-lying Co2(CO)8 structures and calculated CO vibrational spectra; Co2(CO)8 therefore has a tractable structural/modal basis.
5. The target rate is experimentally observed rather than generated by the proposed CSA variables.

### Failure / blocker
The experimentally visible CO stretches are spectroscopic reporters of isomer identity. The current evidence does not establish that any one measured CO-stretch carrier is the mechanical reaction-coordinate mode that carries the bridge-opening/closing motion. Exact isomer-specific T1 values sufficient to form a licensed same-carrier chi have not been recovered from an accessible primary numerical table. The Eyring barrier derived from the same exchange rates cannot be used as an independent validation barrier.

### Failure investigation
The missing pieces are in principle computable, but not safely inferable. A defensible rescue requires:
- recover exact isomer- and solvent-specific CO-stretch T1 values from the original 2D-IR data;
- choose an independent electronic-structure PES/barrier rather than the Eyring barrier;
- calculate the mass-weighted overlap between each experimentally tracked reactant mode and the independent transition-state/reaction-path subspace.
If those overlaps are small, the correct result is that the reporter is not the reactive carrier. That would be a scientific negative result, not a failed computation.

## Candidate 3: HONO in solid Kr

### Successes
1. Direct OH-stretch pumping produces cis-trans isomerization in the same matrix experiment.
2. The cis excited population decays on about 20 ps and the initial reported cis-to-trans channel has approximately 10% yield on the same timescale (Schanz et al. 2005, DOI 10.1063/1.1834567).
3. Longer-time work finds a slower approximately 2 ns isomerization channel and approximately 20 ns final vibrational cooling (Botan et al. 2006, DOI 10.1063/1.2204914).
4. The OH-stretch lifetime and final cooling are reported to vary only weakly with temperature from roughly 15-30 K while quantum yield changes substantially (Botan & Hamm 2008, DOI 10.1063/1.2978386).
5. Full-dimensional gas-phase calculations and matrix-oriented models show strong mode specificity, torsional participation, and matrix translation/cage involvement (Richter et al. 2004, DOI 10.1063/1.1632471; 10.1063/1.1651051).

### Failure / blocker
The observed disappearance of the pumped OH-stretch population is not a clean independent bath-damping measurement for the same reactive carrier. It contains IVR and a reactive branch on comparable timescales. Using that decay directly as gamma and then testing it against the same isomerization outcome would contaminate the independence gate. Furthermore, the matrix-assisted pathway explicitly involves torsional and cage-translation coordinates, so the pumped OH stretch is not equivalent to the reaction coordinate.

### Failure investigation
This failure is mechanistic and informative. The literature indicates a multi-stage pathway: OH-stretch preparation -> IVR/resonance -> torsional/delocalized states -> cage-assisted transfer -> product, followed by long cooling. The fact that reaction yield changes strongly with temperature while the OH-stretch lifetime changes little is direct evidence against treating one scalar OH-stretch damping ratio as the controlling reaction coordinate. HONO therefore supports the layered chi/Capital-Chi distinction even though it does not provide an independent four-layer scalar closure.

## Candidate 4: photoinduced electron-transfer GLE

### Successes
1. The reaction coordinate is explicitly defined as solvent/solute polarization.
2. The source uses an inertial generalized Langevin equation on that coordinate.
3. The free-energy surface is obtained independently from stationary spectroscopy.
4. The friction kernel is independently calibrated using a nonreactive Coumarin-153 probe.
5. The time-resolved target dynamics are not used to fit the FES or friction.
6. No fit parameters enter the final GLE prediction within the stated applicability limits (Angulo et al. 2017, DOI 10.1063/1.4990044).

### Failure / refusal
The friction is explicitly non-Markovian:
[
\eta(t)=\omega_L^2\gamma\delta(t)+\omega_L^2\sum_i k_i e^{-\lambda_i t}.
]
The memory terms are part of the physics and become important in the regimes where the simpler diffusive/Markovian reduction performs worse. Therefore a single local gamma/(2 omega0) does not exhaust the carrier dynamics and is not licensed as the lowercase CSA scalar for this closure test.

### Failure investigation
This is not missing-data failure. It is a model-class refusal. The correct CSA representation is to retain the memory kernel in the native layer and, if desired, embed the exponential kernel terms in an enlarged Markovian state space for Capital-Chi/modal bookkeeping. The refusal is evidence that CSA's scalar gate prevents exactly the over-reduction criticized in the original manuscript.

## Cross-candidate conclusion

The search did not produce a FULL_CLOSE. It did produce four distinct and scientifically useful outcomes:

1. CO/Cu(001): nearly closable empirical surface case; remaining gaps are condition matching and quantitative modal projection.
2. Co2(CO)8: structurally promising molecular case; present blocker is reporter-versus-reactive-carrier identity plus inaccessible exact T1 data.
3. HONO/Kr: real mode-selective chemistry that fails scalar independence because the pumped-mode decay is entangled with the reaction network.
4. Photoinduced ET GLE: native end-to-end dynamics that correctly refuses scalar reduction because memory is essential.

This means the missing matched example is not one generic absence. The four failures separate into four different causes: condition/provenance, carrier identity, outcome contamination, and non-Markovian model class. Those causes should be reported separately rather than collapsed into 'no dataset found'.
