# H/Ru(0001) Experimental Opportunity and Literature-Collision Map v0.1

**Date:** 14 September 2026  
**Status:** EXPERIMENT-DESIGN-FIRST MAP WITH LITERATURE COLLISION  
**Program authority:** SymC General Operations Manual v0.8.0

## Method

The experimental questions below are framed first from the unresolved System-3 claims, as though the program were going to perform the experiments. Only after defining what each experiment would measure is the literature used as a collision test. A published experiment that already answers the frozen question is treated as a benefit and an independent validation target, not as lost novelty.

The literature search is not claimed exhaustive. "No matched study found" means no directly matched public source was identified in the current search, not proof that none exists.

## E1. Clean Ru(0001) interlayer relaxation

### Experiment designed from the project question

Use quantitative LEED-IV or surface X-ray diffraction on clean Ru(0001), with controlled hydrogen exposure as a contamination/perturbation arm, to measure the top several Ru interlayer spacings and their temperature dependence. Compare directly with the relaxed clean-surface geometry generated by System 3.

### Why it matters

This would independently test whether the DFT surface-relaxation convention reproduces the physical Ru substrate before H adsorption is used to validate anything downstream.

### Literature collision

**Already substantially answered.** Published LEED-IV and surface X-ray diffraction studies report a roughly 2% contraction of the outermost clean Ru(0001) spacing at room temperature, investigate hydrogen exposure, and document the long-standing difference between some early ab initio predictions and experiment.

Key collision sources:

- "Relaxation of the clean, Cu- and H-covered Ru(0001) surface," Surface Science 302 (1994) 81-92.
- "Relaxation and thermal expansion of Ru(0001) between 300 and 1870 K and the influence of hydrogen," Surface Science 498 (2002) 74-82, DOI 10.1016/S0039-6028(01)01657-0.

### Disposition

`NOVEL_EXPERIMENT_PRIORITY = LOW`

`INDEPENDENT_VALIDATION_VALUE = HIGH`

Once System 3 relaxation is complete, compare its first three interlayer shifts against these measurements without tuning the relaxation settings to fit them.

## E2. H/D adsorption-site and vibrational-structure mapping

### Experiment designed from the project question

Prepare low-coverage through near-saturation H and D on Ru(0001). Use high-resolution electron-energy-loss spectroscopy (HREELS) with angular/momentum dependence, supplemented by diffraction/coverage control, to identify perpendicular and parallel adsorbate modes, their isotope shifts, dispersion, and coverage dependence. Where possible, use structural/diffraction constraints to discriminate fcc and hcp occupancy.

### Why it matters

This would independently test the adsorption minima, local Hessian/vibrational modes, isotope response, lateral coupling, and the coverage regime in which a local-mode description remains coherent.

### Literature collision

**Much of the basic experiment already exists.** A 2004 high-resolution HREELS study of H and D on Ru(0001) reports narrow perpendicular and doubly degenerate parallel vibrations over a substantial coverage range, isotope-shifted D modes, and momentum-dependent splitting/dispersion of the parallel branch.

Source:

- "Hydrogen on Ru(001) revisited: vibrational structure, adsorption states, and lateral coupling," Surface Science 560 (2004) 130-144, DOI 10.1016/j.susc.2004.04.025.

### Disposition

`NOVEL_BASIC_MODE_MAPPING = LOW`

`INDEPENDENT_VALIDATION_VALUE = HIGH`

`POSSIBLE_OPEN_EXTENSION = MODE_LINEWIDTH/LIFETIME_AND_DEPHASING_DECOMPOSITION`

The literature found in this search establishes frequencies and coupling far more clearly than it establishes the independent lifetime/dephasing decomposition required to turn a linewidth into mechanical damping.

## E3. Temperature-dependent atomic H diffusion

### Experiment designed from the project question

Use helium spin-echo (HeSE) to measure intermediate scattering functions over multiple momentum transfers and crystallographic directions across approximately 70-300 K. Infer site-to-site jump rates, fcc/hcp energy asymmetry, multiple-jump behavior, and the crossover from activated hopping to tunneling.

### Why it matters

This is the direct experimental observable needed to test the final computed H/Ru diffusion dynamics without using experiment to choose the upstream DFT substrate or path.

### Literature collision

**Already performed in unusually strong form.** McIntosh et al. measured atomic H motion on Ru(0001) between 75 and 250 K with HeSE. The work reports quantum effects to about 200 K, a tunneling-dominated low-temperature plateau around 1.9 x 10^9 s^-1, and a Bayesian fit of the 250 K data yielding an fcc-hcp site-energy difference of 22.2 +/- 0.6 meV. The low-q behavior is interpreted as multiple jumps and low adsorbate-substrate friction.

Source:

- McIntosh et al., "Quantum Effects in the Diffusion of Hydrogen on Ru(0001)," J. Phys. Chem. Lett. 4 (2013) 1565-1569, DOI 10.1021/jz400622v. Open-access full text is available.

### Disposition

`NOVEL_H_RATE_CURVE_PRIORITY = LOW`

`INDEPENDENT_VALIDATION_VALUE = VERY_HIGH`

This is a prime external validation target for the final System-3 path/rate tier.

## E4. Matched H-versus-D diffusion under the same HeSE protocol

### Experiment designed from the project question

Repeat the HeSE diffusion measurement for D under the same Ru crystal, coverage, momentum-transfer grid, and temperature range as H. Fit H and D with the same kinetic architecture and report the isotope dependence of the site asymmetry, activated branch, tunneling plateau/crossover, and multiple-jump signature.

### Why it matters

A matched isotope pair is much harder for a deficient PES or nuclear-quantum approximation to mimic accidentally. It would discriminate classical barrier errors, zero-point effects, tunneling, and dissipation/memory assumptions more strongly than the H curve alone.

### Literature collision

The current search found extensive H/D Ru(0001) spectroscopy and H2/D2/HD femtosecond desorption work, but **did not identify a directly matched D-adatom HeSE diffusion curve equivalent to the 2013 H data**.

### Disposition

`CURRENTLY_IDENTIFIED_OPPORTUNITY = HIGH`

`LITERATURE_COLLISION_STATUS = NO_DIRECT_MATCH_FOUND_IN_CURRENT_SEARCH`

This should be re-searched before any novelty claim.

## E5. Direct quantitative adsorbate-friction / memory test on the H diffusion coordinate

### Experiment designed from the project question

Acquire high-statistics HeSE ISFs for H and preferably D over a dense low-momentum-transfer grid, multiple temperatures, and more than one well-controlled dilute coverage. Fit the full jump-length distribution and correlation decay with nested dynamical models: Markovian jump diffusion, generalized Langevin/memory-kernel variants, and models constrained by the independently computed PES. Pre-register which observables discriminate friction/memory rather than simply allowing a better flexible fit.

Where experimentally feasible, combine this with an independent adsorbate-mode lifetime measurement so translational diffusion and local vibrational dissipation do not have to stand in for each other.

### Why it matters

The present HeSE literature supports "low friction" qualitatively from multiple jumps, but System 3 ultimately needs a quantitative, mode/coordinate-matched dissipative object if it is to make a numerical damping claim.

### Literature collision

The 2013 HeSE work already contains a low-friction signature, but does not report the numerical projected friction coefficient required here. H/Ru electronic-friction studies exist for H2 dissociation/scattering and laser-driven associative desorption, but those are different coordinates. No public source found in the current search directly closes this quantitative H-adatom diffusion-friction question.

### Disposition

`CURRENTLY_IDENTIFIED_OPPORTUNITY = VERY_HIGH`

This is the most direct experimental opportunity identified by the present System-3 Limit Map.

## E6. Adsorbate vibrational linewidth with independent lifetime/dephasing separation

### Experiment designed from the project question

Measure the H and D adsorbate vibrational bands on Ru(0001) at controlled low coverage over temperature with sufficient spectral resolution to track homogeneous linewidth, while independently measuring or bounding the population lifetime. Where an echo or time-domain analogue is feasible, use it to separate population relaxation from pure dephasing. Repeat across momentum transfer where lateral coupling splits/disperses the parallel modes.

### Why it matters

This would test the exact spectroscopy firewall used by ChemSA: a linewidth is not a mechanical damping coefficient until lifetime, pure-dephasing, orientational/lateral-coupling, and inhomogeneous contributions are separated appropriately.

### Literature collision

High-resolution HREELS has already mapped H/D frequencies, dispersion, and lateral coupling on Ru(0001). The current search did not identify a directly matched independent T1/Tphi decomposition for these Ru-H adsorbate modes.

### Disposition

`CURRENTLY_IDENTIFIED_OPPORTUNITY = MODERATE_TO_HIGH`

It is especially useful for the spectroscopy side of ChemSA, but it is not a substitute for diffusion-coordinate friction.

## E7. Hot-electron / associative-desorption friction experiments

### Experiment designed from the project question

Use femtosecond pump-pump or pump-probe experiments on H/D-covered Ru(0001) to measure isotope-dependent associative H2/D2/HD desorption yields, energy partitioning, fluence dependence, and two-pulse correlations, comparing directly against multidimensional electronic-friction simulations.

### Literature collision

**Already a mature experimental/theoretical line.** Multiple studies have compared femtosecond laser-induced H2/D2/HD desorption from Ru(0001) with multidimensional DFT-based electronic-friction dynamics, including isotope effects and energy partitioning.

Representative sources include:

- J. Chem. Phys. 2006, DOI 10.1063/1.2206588;
- Phys. Chem. Chem. Phys. 2011, DOI 10.1039/C0CP02086A;
- Phys. Rev. B 95, 125439 (2017), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevB.95.125439;
- J. Phys. Chem. C 2023, DOI 10.1021/acs.jpcc.3c02941.

### Disposition

`NOVEL_BASIC_EXPERIMENT_PRIORITY = LOW`

`METHODOLOGICAL_FRICTION_BENCHMARK_VALUE = HIGH`

These results validate that H/Ru dissipation is physically important and computable in some coordinates, but they cannot be transplanted into the thermal H-adatom diffusion coordinate.

## Priority order for System 3

1. **Matched quantitative H-adatom diffusion friction/memory test** after the PES/path is independently fixed.
2. **Matched D HeSE diffusion curve** under the H experiment's conditions.
3. **H/D vibrational lifetime/dephasing decomposition** on Ru(0001).
4. Existing **H HeSE diffusion curve** as independent external rate validation.
5. Existing **clean-surface XRD/LEED** as independent relaxation validation.
6. Existing **H/D HREELS** as independent local-mode/coverage validation.
7. Existing **femtosecond desorption/electronic-friction** studies as method-adjacent dissipation benchmarks.

## Program consequence

The literature collision is favorable to the research program. Several expensive experiments that would otherwise be needed to validate the Ru substrate, H adsorption modes, and H diffusion curve have already been performed. The largest remaining gap is narrower and more informative: a **quantitative dissipative object matched to the same adsorbed-H diffusion coordinate**, with isotope and memory sensitivity strong enough to challenge the eventual first-principles model.
