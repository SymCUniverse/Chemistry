\paragraph{Validated-substrate inheritance and its boundary.}
A qualified clean-surface representation is treated as reusable numerical infrastructure, not as a transferable chemical result. Once a specific substrate System Model has passed the thickness, vacuum, reciprocal-space, force, coupled-sensitivity, and independent-reproduction gates required by its own protocol, a compatible downstream calculation may inherit that substrate certificate when the substrate-defining physical and computational assumptions remain materially unchanged. What is inherited is the bounded numerical certificate and its provenance. Adsorption-site ordering, lateral-cell convergence, reaction paths, saddle properties, vibrational modes, friction, nuclear-quantum effects, rates, and any ChemSA stability classification remain separately qualified.

Under the P0-Q qualification rules, an observed numerical HOLD may be used to design a versioned successor substrate representation, but the parent HOLD remains part of the scientific record and the evidence used to design the successor is not subsequently described as untouched confirmation. The certificate therefore belongs to the exact version that actually passes, and its scope is limited by both the demonstrated Function Map and the observed Limit Map. Transfer is held if a downstream calculation changes a substrate-defining element of the Hamiltonian or boundary conditions, enters a materially different surface state, or reveals sensitivity outside the qualified region. A different material or facet, reconstruction, defect, alloying, oxidation, substantial strain, charge or field, explicit solvent, altered electrostatic treatment, or an adsorbate-induced numerical sensitivity outside the certificate requires targeted requalification rather than silent inheritance. At the present evidence state, neither the active CO/Cu(111) P0-Q continuation nor the H/Ru(0001) clean-surface route has issued a reusable final substrate certificate; for H/Ru(0001), the deeper layer extension passed but the required coupled recheck remains historically on HOLD because of the k-mesh axis.
